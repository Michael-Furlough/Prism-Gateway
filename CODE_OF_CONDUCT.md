# Prism Gateway Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

Prism Gateway is built by people who care about getting security-critical infrastructure right — which requires disagreement, scrutiny, and rigorous review. This code of conduct exists to make sure that rigor stays aimed at the work, not at the people doing it.

We are committed to making participation in this project — as a contributor, reviewer, maintainer, issue reporter, or community member in any official space — a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

This applies across every space associated with the project: GitHub issues, pull requests, discussions, RFC threads, official chat channels, and any in-person or virtual event organized under the Prism Gateway name.

## 1. Our Philosophy

Security review, by nature, involves telling people their code has a problem — sometimes a serious one, sometimes repeatedly, sometimes about a design they're personally attached to. We think this can and should be done without being unkind, and that a project can hold an extremely high technical bar while still being a place people want to keep showing up to.

Two things we explicitly reject as false tradeoffs:

- **"Rigorous review requires bluntness that reads as harsh."** It doesn't. The clearest, most useful reviews we've seen are also respectful ones — the two are not in tension, and confusing bluntness with rigor usually just means the *reviewer* skipped the harder work of being both clear and kind.
- **"A welcoming community means lowering the bar."** It doesn't. We hold every contribution — from a first-time contributor's typo fix to a maintainer's core pipeline change — to the same standard described in `CONTRIBUTING.md`. Welcoming means the *feedback process* is respectful and constructive, not that the *standard* is negotiable.

## 2. Expected Behavior

- **Engage with the work, not the person.** Critique ideas, designs, and code directly and specifically; avoid framing disagreement in terms of the other person's competence, intelligence, or judgment.
- **Assume good faith by default.** Most mistakes in a technical project are mistakes, not bad intent. Ask clarifying questions before assuming the worst interpretation of an ambiguous comment or a rejected suggestion.
- **Be specific and actionable in feedback.** "This doesn't handle the depth-limit case correctly" is useful. "This is sloppy" is not, even if the underlying technical concern is valid — say the technical concern instead.
- **Accept feedback gracefully, and give people room to be wrong.** Everyone — including maintainers — ships something imperfect sometimes. The response to a valid critique is to fix it or explain your reasoning, not to become defensive.
- **Respect people's time and effort**, especially from volunteers and first-time contributors. If you can't review something soon, a short "I'll get to this by [timeframe]" is worth more than silence.
- **Disclose conflicts of interest** relevant to a review or decision (e.g., reviewing a PR from your own employer that specifically benefits your employer's use case) so others can weigh input appropriately.
- **Use welcoming, inclusive language.** Assume readers of any background, and avoid idioms, jokes, or references that only land for a narrow cultural or demographic group.
- **Own your mistakes without spiraling.** If you get something wrong — a bad review comment, a merged PR that broke something — acknowledge it plainly, fix what needs fixing, and move on. We'd rather see quick, honest correction than either defensiveness or excessive self-flagellation.

## 3. Unacceptable Behavior

- Harassment in any form, including unwelcome comments related to personal characteristics listed in "Our Commitment," and unwelcome sexual attention or advances.
- Insulting, demeaning, or dismissive comments directed at a person rather than their work ("this is stupid," "did you even read the docs," or similar, regardless of how frustrating the underlying situation is).
- Trolling, deliberately inflammatory comments, or sustained derailment of technical discussions.
- Public or private harassment, including targeted follow-up across multiple threads or channels after a disagreement has been resolved or a maintainer has asked it to stop.
- Publishing others' private information (physical or email address, etc.) without explicit permission — this includes doxxing in the context of a disagreement about a contribution.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, reviews, or community spaces, including repeatedly re-litigating a decision after it has gone through the process described in `GOVERNANCE.md`.
- Advocacy for, or minimization of, any of the above, including "just joking" framing used to excuse otherwise unacceptable behavior.
- Impersonating a maintainer, security team member, or other contributor.
- Submitting reports under this policy in bad faith, as a tool to harass or retaliate against another contributor.

## 4. Scope

This code of conduct applies within all official project spaces (GitHub repository interactions, official chat/discussion platforms, project-run events), and also applies when an individual is officially representing the project in a public space (e.g., speaking about Prism Gateway at a conference, posting from an official project account).

It does not extend to unrelated personal conduct in spaces unconnected to the project — but conduct in adjacent spaces that directly and demonstrably affects someone's safety or ability to participate in the project may still be considered, at maintainer discretion, per §6.

## 5. Reporting

If you experience or witness behavior that violates this code of conduct:

- **Email**: `conduct@prism-gateway.dev` — reaches the maintainer team members who are not the subject of the report, structured so a report about one maintainer doesn't route through that same person.
- **Direct message** to any maintainer, if you have an established relationship and prefer that channel — they will loop in the conduct-handling group appropriately.

A report should include, as available: what happened, where, when, who was involved, and any relevant links or screenshots. You do not need to have a complete picture to report — a partial account is enough to start looking into it.

**We will not publicly identify a reporter without their explicit consent**, and will take reasonable steps to protect reporter confidentiality throughout any investigation, consistent with the need to fairly investigate the report.

---

## 6. Enforcement

Reports are handled by maintainers who are not directly involved in the incident. The process is:

1. **Acknowledge** the report, typically within a few days.
2. **Investigate**, which may include reviewing the referenced conversation, reaching out to the reporter for clarification, and — where appropriate and fair — hearing the other party's account.
3. **Decide** on a response, guided by the severity framework in §7.
4. **Communicate** the outcome to the reporter (to the extent consistent with the privacy of all parties involved) and, where the action affects them, to the person whose conduct was reported.
5. **Follow up**, particularly for repeated or serious violations, to confirm the resolution actually addressed the behavior.

We aim to act proportionately, consistently, and without unnecessary delay. We also recognize that conduct reports can be sensitive on all sides, and we take care not to treat an accusation as automatically equivalent to a finding.

---

## 7. Enforcement Guidelines

| Level | Example | Typical Response |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Correction** | A single instance of unwelcoming language or a sharp-but-not-abusive comment | Private, direct feedback from a maintainer; usually no further action if acknowledged |
| **2 — Warning** | A pattern of dismissive/demeaning comments, or a single more serious incident | Formal private warning, with a clear statement of expected change; may include a temporary cooldown on specific interactions (e.g., pausing review exchanges between two specific people) |
| **3 — Temporary restriction** | Continued violation after a warning, or a serious single incident (harassment, doxxing attempt) | Temporary ban from project spaces (issues, PRs, discussions, chat) for a defined period |
| **4 — Permanent removal** | Severe violations, or repeated violations after prior restriction | Permanent ban from all project spaces; maintainer/contributor status revoked where applicable |

These are guidelines, not a rigid formula — context matters, including severity, pattern, whether the person acknowledged the issue, and impact on the person(s) affected. Maintainers use judgment, and this table exists to keep that judgment roughly consistent over time rather than to replace it with a lookup table.

## 8. Maintainer Responsibilities

Maintainers are held to a **higher**, not lower, standard under this code of conduct, because their conduct sets the tone for how the whole project treats disagreement:

- Maintainers are expected to model the behavior in §2 consistently, including in high-friction moments like rejecting a contribution someone put real effort into.
- Maintainers are responsible for enforcing this code of conduct fairly and consistently across all contributors, including other maintainers and, if necessary, themselves recognizing and correcting their own conduct.
- Maintainers who repeatedly fail to meet the standards in this document may have their maintainer status reviewed through the process in `GOVERNANCE.md`, independent of their technical contributions — technical skill does not exempt anyone from this policy.
- Maintainers handling a conduct report involving themselves must recuse from that specific investigation and decision.

---

## 9. Appeals

Anyone subject to an enforcement action may appeal by emailing `conduct@prism-gateway.dev` with a request for review, ideally within 30 days of the action. The appeal will be reviewed by maintainer(s) not involved in the original decision where project size allows; if that isn't possible given the size of the maintainer team, the original decision-makers will re-review with explicit attention to any new information or perspective raised in the appeal.
